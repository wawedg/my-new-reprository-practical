What is the Pomodoro Technique?

The Pomodoro Technique is a time-management method that splits work into short, focused intervals called pomodoros, with short breaks between them. It helps you start difficult tasks, keep your attention on them, and avoid burning out.

Where it comes from

Francesco Cirillo came up with the method in the late 1980s, when he was a university student. He used a tomato-shaped kitchen timer to time his study sessions, and that is where the name comes from: pomodoro is Italian for “tomato”.

How it works

  1. Choose a task you want to work on.
  2. Set the timer to 25 minutes.
  3. Work on the task and nothing else until the timer rings.
  4. Take a short break of about 5 minutes when it rings.
  5. Repeat. Each 25-minute work block is one pomodoro.
  6. Take a longer break of 15–30 minutes after every four pomodoros.

Why it helps

  • Easier to start. Committing to 25 minutes feels much smaller than “finish the whole assignment”.
  • Fewer distractions. A visible countdown makes it easier to hold off on checking your phone.
  • Less mental fatigue. Regular breaks keep your focus steady over long study sessions.
  • Better estimates. Counting pomodoros per task shows you how long things really take.

Tips for students

  • Break big assignments into tasks that take one to four pomodoros each.
  • If something distracting comes to mind, write it down and get back to work.
  • Use breaks to actually rest: stand up, stretch, drink water, and stay away from screens.
  • If you get interrupted for a long time, restart the pomodoro instead of counting it.
